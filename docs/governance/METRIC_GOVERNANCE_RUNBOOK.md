# Metric governance and outcome learning runbook

Version: 1.0.0  
Card: `ecf1148c`  
Machine-readable owner register: `docs/governance/metric-governance.v1.json`

## 1. Purpose and invariants

This runbook governs every production metric family, immutable report, forecast,
AI evaluation, recommendation outcome, user task measure, and accessibility
regression. The named roles in the owner register are operational role names.
The accountable owner must keep a current person-to-role assignment in the
approved service catalog. A vacant role makes its product unapproved, not
implicitly owned by an implementer or model.

The process is aggregate, evidence-led, and resistant to gaming:

1. Canonical metrics are calculated deterministically. Models may explain or
   propose, but do not calculate canonical values, approve changes, or close
   decisions.
2. No review may rank a person, user, or agent. Prohibited dimensions in the
   owner register apply to metrics, forecasts, AI evaluations, reports, and
   backlog priority calculations.
3. Acceptance is a disposition, not proof of correctness. Verified effect
   requires an approved outcome window, baseline or comparison, and independent
   evidence.
4. Unknown, stale, partial, unavailable, unreachable, and not applicable remain
   distinct from measured zero. Missing evidence is never inferred from a card
   lifecycle state, a link, or silence.
5. Decisions are append-only. Corrections and retirements add superseding
   records and retain the prior record and evidence.
6. Review records contain bounded references and hashes, not credentials,
   sensitive raw prompts, protected source payloads, or individual telemetry.

## 2. Ownership and cadence

The machine-readable owner register is the coverage authority. It assigns an
accountable owner, data owner or review owner, and cadence to all 12 production
metric families plus these product classes:

- production reports;
- probabilistic forecasts;
- AI evaluations;
- recommendation outcomes;
- UX task measures; and
- accessibility regressions.

Cadence is a maximum interval. Event-driven reviews happen sooner when an
owner, definition, target, exclusion, threshold, source adapter, model, pricing
revision, task protocol, accessibility baseline, or golden fixture changes.
The Metric Governance Steward performs a monthly coverage check. Any production
item absent from the register is quarantined from target reporting until a
versioned owner entry is approved.

Required meeting independence:

- the proposer may present evidence but may not be the sole approver;
- the data owner confirms lineage and data quality;
- the accountable owner accepts operational meaning and balancing measures;
- the named review owner challenges calibration, harm, accessibility, and
  gaming risk; and
- protected-data or policy changes also require the existing authorization and
  legal approval path. This runbook grants no authority.

## 3. Versioned change control

Definition, target, exclusion, threshold, and deprecation changes use one
change proposal containing every `required_change_fields` entry in the owner
register. The proposed version follows semantic versioning:

- major for changed meaning, population, unit, polarity, or incompatible method;
- minor for an additive dimension, approved target, threshold, or balancing
  measure that preserves prior interpretation;
- patch for clarification with byte-identical calculation and fixtures.

A proposal cannot become effective until:

1. the data owner reproduces the current fixture and records source coverage,
   errors, exclusions, watermark, and evidence hashes;
2. proposed calculation output is generated by the deterministic engine;
3. every affected golden fixture is updated, parsed, and hash-pinned, including
   boundary, zero, no-value, exclusion, and adverse cases;
4. the accountable owner and data owner append explicit approvals;
5. an independent reviewer confirms interpretation, balancing measures,
   retirement triggers, and non-individual scope; and
6. the decision record names the old and new versions, effective time, report
   and forecast compatibility, migration treatment, and rollback or retirement
   plan.

No in-place edit is approval. A correction appends a record with
`supersedes_decision_id`. Historical reports retain their original registry,
fixture, model, pricing, and task-protocol versions. A deprecated definition
remains resolvable for reproduction and cannot silently feed current targets.

## 4. Operating cycle

### 4.1 Intake and data-quality review

At each cadence, the steward freezes a review window and records the exact
registry, fixtures, source adapters, report hashes, forecast method, model and
evaluation set, recommendation practice versions, UX protocol, and
accessibility baseline. The data owner reports population coverage, missingness,
duplicates, late data, freshness, clock and cohort consistency, exclusion
volume and reason, source errors, denominator validity, and drift. Report both
counts and rates so a changing denominator is visible.

If quality is below an approved threshold, publish the typed failure state and
abstain from target or effect claims. Do not impute health or zero.

### 4.2 Target and threshold review

A target must state purpose, baseline, population, window, polarity, confidence,
owner, expected behavior, balancing measures, and a sunset date. Thresholds
must define warning, breach, and recovery behavior. Owners inspect distribution
and segment-level harm in authorized aggregate cohorts, not only the estate
average. Targets designed mainly to reward hitting the target are rejected.

### 4.3 Forecast calibration review

Use only outcomes that became knowable after forecast issuance. Keep forecast
and outcome timestamps, cohort, horizon, probability range, assumptions,
exclusions, misses, drift, and abstentions. Review at least:

- empirical coverage for each published probability range;
- interval width and directional bias alongside coverage;
- P95 misses and material schedule harm;
- calibration by approved aggregate cohort and horizon;
- changed assumptions and dependency sensitivity;
- drift and insufficient-sample abstention quality; and
- the rate of forecasts issued when the method should have abstained.

Do not convert a range to a promised date. Do not estimate or rank individual
capacity. Recalibration needs a versioned method proposal and fixture update.

### 4.4 AI evaluation and recommendation outcome review

Join a recommendation or abstention to a later outcome through explicit IDs and
separate evidence records. Never infer a result from acceptance, lifecycle
state, or a link alone. Review the following as separate measures:

- calibration of stated confidence and expected impact ranges;
- accepted, rejected, expired, and no-action dispositions;
- override rate, typed override reason, and whether the override prevented or
  introduced harm;
- verified beneficial, neutral, harmful, inconclusive, and not-yet-verifiable
  effect after the declared horizon;
- harmful false positives, including severity and reversibility;
- abstention quality, including appropriate abstentions and harmful failures to
  abstain;
- rework incidence, effort band, delay, and cause;
- cost per accepted outcome and cost per independently verified beneficial
  outcome, with pricing revision and confidence; and
- counter-indicators, citation and evidence validity, user feedback, and
  downstream accessibility effects.

Measure acceptance and cost together with verified effect, harm, override, and
rework. Never optimize acceptance alone. Evaluation sets include golden,
adversarial, abstention, stale-evidence, policy-denied, accessibility, and
counter-indicator cases. Model or prompt promotion requires the named
independent AI risk review and cannot erase prior results.

### 4.5 UX task success and accessibility regression

The UX Research Steward maintains versioned common-task protocols with start
and success definitions, allowed assistance, device and viewport, keyboard
path, click count, elapsed time, error and recovery, abandonment, and evidence
reachability. Report task success with error, abandonment, time distribution,
and accessibility measures, not speed alone.

The Accessibility Steward compares each UI change and the monthly production
sample against the approved WCAG 2.2 AA automation and manual baseline,
keyboard and focus path, accessible names and landmarks, contrast, non-color
states, reduced motion, zoom, responsive layouts, reviewed screenshots, and
known limitations. Any material regression blocks claims of UX improvement and
enters the backlog regardless of faster task time.

## 5. Goodhart controls and retirement triggers

Each governed measure must name a balancing measure and retirement triggers.
The minimum controls are:

| Primary measure | Balancing measures | Retire, quarantine, or redesign when |
|---|---|---|
| Delivery or flow speed | quality, escaped defects, rework, blocked age, user outcome | speed improves while quality or rework materially worsens for two windows; scope or timestamp gaming is confirmed |
| Target attainment | distribution, denominator and exclusions, quality, harm | exclusion or denominator changes drive the result; target saturation removes decision value |
| Forecast coverage | interval width, bias, misses, abstention, usefulness | trivially wide intervals meet coverage; material drift persists for two reviews; sample is insufficient |
| Recommendation acceptance | verified effect, harmful false positives, override, rework | acceptance rises without verified benefit; harm threshold is breached; owners feel pressured to accept |
| Cost per accepted outcome | cost per verified benefit, quality, harm, abstention | cheaper outcomes increase rework or harm; pricing or attribution cannot be reproduced |
| UX task time | success, error, abandonment, accessibility, comprehension | speed gains cause lower success, inaccessible paths, or material regression |
| Accessibility conformance | manual task success, assistive-technology evidence, known limitations | automation passes while manual critical tasks fail; baseline no longer covers production UI |

Universal triggers also apply: broken lineage, unreproducible fixtures, owner
vacancy, unapproved definition drift, persistent stale or partial data,
individual-ranking leakage, a metric becoming a reward proxy, or no recorded decision use for three scheduled reviews. Quarantine stops target reporting.
Retirement appends an effective date, rationale, replacement if any, impacted
reports, retention period, and final fixture hashes. It never deletes history.

Stewards inspect suspicious boundary clustering, sudden exclusion growth,
missing unfavorable outcomes, interval inflation, label churn, proxy migration,
and disagreement between primary and balancing measures. Confirmed gaming is a
system-design finding, not an individual performance score.

## 6. Append-only decision log

The approved CardStore or governance event store is the decision system of
record. Use its supported append command. If an authorized writer must append
JSONL directly, it must:

1. create an object with a JSON serializer, never string concatenation;
2. acquire the store's supported lock;
3. parse every existing nonblank line before writing;
4. validate the event schema and unique decision ID;
5. write exactly one serialized line and `fsync`; and
6. parse every line again after append.

Structural events such as create, claim, move, complete, and link remain separate from evidence events. A verdict or verified effect requires an
explicit evidence event with evidence hashes. Neither lifecycle state nor link
existence supplies a verdict.

Each decision event records at least: decision ID, event version, subject and
subject version, decision type, proposal ID, outcome, rationale, actor role,
independent reviewer role, review window, registry and fixture hashes, bounded
evidence references and hashes, balancing measure results, retirement-trigger
status, effective time, and optional superseded decision ID. Corrections append;
they do not rewrite.

## 7. Evidence-backed improvement backlog

Every unresolved quality, calibration, harm, abstention, override, rework,
cost, UX, accessibility, gaming, or retirement finding becomes a backlog event.
It includes:

- immutable finding ID and source decision ID;
- problem statement and affected governed product;
- evidence references and hashes;
- baseline and balancing-measure impact;
- severity and reversibility;
- confidence and uncertainty;
- accountable owner and review-by date;
- dependency IDs and explicit blocked reason, if any;
- proposed experiment or correction and expected evidence; and
- priority inputs.

Prioritize reproducibly by harm severity, affected aggregate scope, evidence
confidence, urgency, reversibility, and effort band. Safety, authorization,
accessibility, harmful false positives, and broken lineage take precedence over
acceptance or speed gains. Do not use individual output, identity, model
preference, or executive sponsorship as a score. Ties are ordered by oldest
finding ID. The monthly decision record publishes the ordered backlog and the
inputs used. Closing an item requires a separate verified-effect event; a code
merge, card completion, or recommendation acceptance alone is insufficient.

## 8. Review checklist

A review is complete only when the append-only decision links:

- owner-register version and current role assignments;
- registry, definition, target, exclusion, threshold, report, forecast, model,
  evaluation-set, task-protocol, accessibility-baseline, and fixture versions
  that apply;
- data-quality and calibration evidence;
- recommendation dispositions and separately verified outcomes;
- harmful false positives, abstention, override, rework, cost, UX task success,
  and accessibility regression results;
- primary and balancing measures plus retirement-trigger decisions; and
- the prioritized evidence-backed backlog.

If evidence is absent, record Unknown or abstention and the backlog item. Do not
manufacture a pass.
